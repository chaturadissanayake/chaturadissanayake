The Long Way Round

If you'd asked teenage me what I'd end up doing, "communications consultant for the UN" was not on the list. I skipped more school than I attended, most of it spent outside the gates rather than in them — less a plan, more a temper. I'd read too much and wanted my own version of the stories on the shelf, so I just left and tried to have one.

Some of that was a boarding house year I didn't love, and a home I wasn't in a hurry to get back to. I won't dress it up as mischief for its own sake. Looking back, most of what read as rebellion then was really just a kid trying to find somewhere to put his energy.

The one thing that stuck from all that unsupervised time was making things — digital collages, cut and pasted for no reason other than it felt good to build something out of nothing. That's how graphic design found me, not really the other way around. A few years into it, sitting at a screen every day, I realised design on its own wasn't the whole plan. I wanted to work on things with actual stakes, go further, be useful somewhere bigger than a client brief. Same instinct, different desk — that's the thread that eventually led to the work on the other page.
